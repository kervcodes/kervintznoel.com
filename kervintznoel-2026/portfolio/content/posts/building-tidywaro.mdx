Every six months someone on engineering Twitter declares DSA study dead. "You'll never reverse a binary tree at work." They're right about the tree. They're wrong about the point.

## What DSA actually trains

The skill isn't the algorithm. It's the habit of asking: *what is the shape of this problem?* Is it a search? A sort? A graph traversal disguised as something else?

That question — asked fast, under pressure — is exactly what incident response demands. When a production system is down, you're doing triage, which is just prioritized search under constraints.

## How I study without burning out

I do one problem a day. Not five. Not ten. One, done well, with a written explanation of my approach. I use the Blind 75 as a curriculum, not a checklist. Some problems I revisit three times before they click.

The goal isn't to memorize solutions. It's to build pattern recognition — so when a real problem arrives, you've seen its shape before.
