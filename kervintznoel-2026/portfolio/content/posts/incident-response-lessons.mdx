Incident response looks clean on a diagram. Detect → triage → mitigate → resolve → postmortem. In practice it's messier, faster, and more human than any runbook prepares you for.

## The postmortem problem

Most postmortems fail before they start. Not because teams aren't smart — because they optimize for documentation over learning. A five-page doc nobody reads is not a postmortem. It's liability coverage.

The best postmortem I ever ran was two whiteboards and thirty minutes. We left with three action items, all owned, all shipped within a week.

## The metric nobody talks about

MTTR gets all the attention. Mean time to resolution — how fast did you fix it? But MTTR hides a more important number: **time to first hypothesis**.

How long did it take your team to have a theory about what was wrong? That gap — alert fires to first guess — is where most incidents are actually won or lost.

## What sustainable on-call looks like

Sustainable on-call isn't about fewer pages. It's about pages that make sense. If your engineers can't explain *why* an alert fired within 60 seconds of looking at it, the alert is wrong — not the engineer.
