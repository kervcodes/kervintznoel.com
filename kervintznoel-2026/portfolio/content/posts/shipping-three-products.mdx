I have a full-time job, two young boys, and three side projects in active development. People ask how I manage it. Honestly? Badly, sometimes. But I have a system that makes "badly" a recoverable state.

## The constraint that changed everything

I stopped trying to work on all three projects every week. Instead I assign each project a primary week on a rotating basis. That week it gets my best hours. The others get maintenance only — bug fixes, dependency updates, nothing new.

This sounds like it would slow everything down. It speeds everything up, because I'm never context-switching at the architectural level.

## The commit rule

Every session ends with a commit. Not a perfect commit. Not a complete feature. A commit. This does two things: it forces me to leave the codebase in a known state, and it gives me a visible record of momentum.

On hard weeks, that git log is the only proof I'm still moving forward. That proof matters more than I expected.

## What I've learned about momentum

Momentum isn't about velocity. It's about not stopping. A small commit every two days beats a massive push every two weeks — for the codebase and for my own motivation.
